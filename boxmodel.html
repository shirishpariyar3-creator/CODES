Forest fires are uncontrolled blazes that burn through vegetation, often spreading rapidly across large areas of woodland. They can start naturally, such as from lightning strikes, or be triggered by human activities like unattended campfires or discarded cigarettes. Once ignited, dry leaves, high temperatures, and strong winds can fuel the fire, making it difficult to contain. While forest fires can cause severe destruction—damaging ecosystems, wildlife habitats, and nearby communities—they also play a natural role in some environments by clearing dead material and allowing new growth. However, due to climate change and increasing human impact, today’s forest fires are becoming more frequent and intense, posing greater risks to both nature and people.
